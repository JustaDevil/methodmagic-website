## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**This does not read as AI-generated. Comfortably.**

**LLM assessment:** No LLM default produces ten hand-built SVG schematics with per-service accent colors, a WebGL particle terrain, a `mask-image` collage cut into the glyphs of "5M+", and copy that says "capsule set" instead of "store assets." The `//` motif is load-bearing — it's the company's own name, so `// THE NUMBERS` reads as brand grammar rather than the tracked-eyebrow reflex. Verdict: bespoke.

Three real tells survive:
- **The Why Us growth chart.** Unlabeled bars, unlabeled axis, a line that only goes up and to the right, `aria-hidden="true"`. It is the generic SaaS growth graphic, and it sits four inches from the words "Data driven / we have the knowledge of what good KPIs are." A fake chart next to a rigor claim is worse than no chart.
- **The marquee.** "QUALITY COMMUNICATIONS // IMPACTFUL MARKETING // WISHLIST GROWTH" is the only copy on the site that could belong to any agency.
- **The `> metric` line** under each service is terminal-costume on a brand that is not a developer tool.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs` returned **1 advisory, 0 errors** — `numbered-section-markers` (01–06). Partial false positive: the Services 01–10 *is* a real enumerated stack, which the rule explicitly permits. It lands on the Why Us list, where `01–05` numbers five unordered claims with no sequence.

**Visual overlays:** Not available. The Browser pane renderer stayed suspended (`document.hidden === true`), so script-injection overlays could not be shown to you. Substituted a headless Chrome pipeline for all visual evidence.

## Overall Impression

This is a good site with a thin skeleton. The hero is confident, the copy is credible in a way most agency sites never manage, and the What We Do section is genuinely excellent — the sticky schematic panel is the kind of thing people screenshot.

Then you count: **14.7 viewport-heights, of which 75% is two sections.** The Numbers spends 4,560px delivering six statistics. Services spends 5,453px delivering ten. Everything a buyer needs to decide — proof, credibility, next step — is compressed into the last 17%.

**The single biggest opportunity:** the site is built to be *admired*, not to be *acted on*. There is no CTA in the hero on desktop and none visible at all on mobile. The only conversion mechanism on the entire site is one `mailto:` link, 12,000px down.

## What's Working

1. **The copy knows the job.** "We've taken pages from low single-digit conversion up to 22% *when the product, traffic, and offer are aligned*" — that qualifier is what a real practitioner writes and a copywriter cuts. Same for "$1–1.5 per wishlist in top markets." A studio marketing lead reads this and recognizes a peer.

2. **The What We Do section is exceptional craft.** Ten purpose-built diagrams, each with its own accent driving the counter color, the caption, and the panel glow simultaneously. The funnel for Steam Intelligence, the cohort-splay for Performance Marketing — these were designed, not assembled.

3. **Body contrast is disciplined.** Measured: active service body 9.69:1, stat captions 6.41:1, SVG labels 7.21:1, nav 4.92:1, button text 6.62:1. This is the single most common failure in dark-mode brand sites and the site avoids it everywhere it matters.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The site has no visible focus indicator — anywhere
Measured: **12 of 12 focusable elements resolve to `outline-style: none`** with no compensating ring. The stylesheet contains zero `:focus` or `:focus-visible` rules. Compounding it, the mobile drawer sits at `translateX(100%)` while staying fully tab-reachable — a keyboard user at ≤860px tabs from the logo into **five invisible links** with no indication of where focus is, no `aria-hidden`, no `aria-controls`, no Esc handler, no focus trap.
**Why it matters:** WCAG 2.4.7 (AA) failure, and a plain functional one — keyboard and switch users cannot navigate. Icelandic company selling into the EU; the European Accessibility Act applies to commercial digital services from June 2025.
**Fix:** Add a global `:focus-visible` ring (2px `--blue-soft` + 2px offset). Set `visibility: hidden` on the closed drawer and flip it in `.open`, add `aria-controls`/`aria-hidden`, Esc-to-close, and focus return to the toggle.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P0] One `mailto:` link is the entire conversion funnel
No hero CTA on desktop. On mobile `.header-cta { display: none }` hides it entirely, so **there is no visible call to action above the fold at all** — the only path is behind a hamburger. The one real CTA sits 12,457px down. `mailto:` opens nothing on a desktop with no configured client, and carries no context (game, stage, platform, timeline).
**Why it matters:** The whole page exists to start conversations, and it makes starting one the hardest thing to do on it.
**Fix:** Put a primary CTA in the hero. Show the header CTA at all widths (drop it beside the hamburger). Add a short qualifying form or a booking link at Contact, keeping the mailto as secondary.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable craft` (hero CTA + contact block)

### [P1] The China Market Engine doesn't load well in China
Service #09 sells native China execution. The site itself depends on **`fonts.googleapis.com`, `fonts.gstatic.com`, and `googletagmanager.com` — all blocked in mainland China** — plus **four render-affecting scripts from `cdn.jsdelivr.net`**, which lost its mainland ICP license in December 2021 and now routes Chinese traffic overseas. The Google Fonts `<link>` is render-blocking in `<head>`, so a mainland visitor waits on a connection that will never open before first paint.
**Why it matters:** A Chinese studio, or a Bilibili/Douyin partner you're pitching, lands on a slow-to-blank page from the company claiming to know that market. It's the one credibility failure the page can't argue its way out of.
**Fix:** Self-host both fonts (~40KB woff2) and self-host or bundle GSAP/Lenis. Test from a mainland vantage point before the next pitch.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable optimize`

### [P1] `prefers-reduced-motion` is declared but doesn't work
Measured with reduce forced on at 1440×900: **74 infinite animations still running.** The media query suppresses seven selectors; it misses every `mm*` keyframe in the What We Do panel (`mmFloat` ×8, `mmFade` ×11, `mmEq` ×9, `mmRing` ×7, `mmDash` ×7, `mmPulse` ×7 …) plus `wvFloat` and `wvPulse`. The JS side is done properly — Lenis, GSAP, WebGL and magnetic buttons all gate on `reduced` correctly — which makes the CSS gap look like an oversight rather than a decision.
Separately, those 74 loops run **continuously for all ten panels even though nine are `opacity: 0`** — nine invisible panels compositing forever.
**Why it matters:** Users who set reduce often do so for vestibular disorders or migraine. This is also constant GPU draw on every laptop that opens the page.
**Fix:** `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) { .wwd-anim *, .why-viz * { animation: none !important; } }`. Independently, add `.wwd-anim:not(.active) * { animation-play-state: paused; }`.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P1] Two sections eat 75% of the page and leave most viewports two-thirds empty
The Numbers = 4,560px for six stats (~760px of scroll each). Services = 5,453px for ten. `.nums-slide` is `align-items: flex-start` with up to 200px of top padding, so at 1440×900 "2% → 22%" and its caption occupy the top third and **the bottom ~60% of the fold is empty**. Same on mobile. `.nums-track` is a hardcoded `height: 4560px` that doesn't scale with viewport.
**Why it matters:** The reel reads as impressive once and expensive by the third stat. A buyer skimming for proof is asked to spend five viewport-heights on six numbers, and every one of those screens is mostly void.
**Fix:** Cut the reel to 3–4 strongest stats and roughly halve the track height (make it viewport-relative, e.g. `calc(100svh * 4)`). Center the slide content vertically, or let the number and caption use the whole fold.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P1] No privacy policy, anywhere
The site runs GA4 with Consent Mode v2 and a cookie banner. Total links on the page: **nine — seven in-page anchors, one mailto, one repeat.** No privacy policy, cookie policy, terms, imprint, or company details. The banner's claim ("No data is sold or shared with third parties") is asserted with nothing to link to.
**Why it matters:** GDPR/ePrivacy require an accessible privacy notice, and consent banners are expected to link to it. Icelandic entity, EEA scope. This is exposure, not polish — and the empty footer costs nothing to fix.
**Fix:** Add `/privacy` and link it from the footer and the banner. Add company name, registration, and a LinkedIn link while you're there.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Inactive service segments render at 1.86:1
`.wwd-seg { opacity: 0.3 }` puts inactive body text at **1.86:1** and inactive titles at **1.67:1** against `--bg`. On desktop, at any scroll position roughly 60% of visible text in that section is illegible. Mobile is fine (`opacity: 1` at ≤900px), so this is desktop-only.
**Why it matters:** Intended as focus, it reads as a rendering bug on first encounter, and it means a reader can't scan ahead.
**Fix:** Raise inactive to `0.55` (≈4.6:1) and carry the focus with the accent color and title weight instead of dimming the prose.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P2] The proof section is the thinnest part of the page
Three logos, no case studies, no named clients beyond Pathfinder in a stat, no testimonials. "Portfolio Studios" appears under Tencent and Krafton but **not FunPlus** — which reads as an oversight and quietly raises the question the caption exists to answer. The hedge itself is doing real work: it means "studios inside their portfolio," but the layout invites the reading "Tencent is our client."
**Why it matters:** Everything above it is confident and specific. The moment a buyer looks for evidence, the page gets vague. This is where deals stall.
**Fix:** Make the caption consistent across all three, or drop it and name the actual studios and titles. One real case study — Pathfinder, with the 2%→22% story — would outperform the entire Numbers reel.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer / studio marketing lead, first visit):** Lands on a hero with no button. Scrolls. Five viewport-heights of numbers with no context for who they belong to. Reaches Services and gets ten of them — no packages, no engagement model, no pricing signal, no "start here." Wants to know what this costs and what happens after emailing; the site answers neither. There is no About, no team, no case study. Abandons at the Numbers reel or bounces off Contact without sending.

**Casey (Mobile, one-handed, on the go):** **No CTA visible above the fold at all** — it's inside the hamburger. Nav links are 27px tall against a 44px minimum; the toggle is 40px; "Accept" on the cookie banner is 38px. Then 12,355px of scroll on a phone, with all ten service animations stripped out — so mobile gets the length of the desktop experience without the payoff. The only reachable CTA is at the very bottom.

**Sam (Screen reader + keyboard):** Tab from the logo, and focus vanishes — no ring on any of the 12 focusables. At ≤860px it disappears into five off-screen drawer links with no `aria-hidden`. Navigating by heading gives: H1, then fifteen H3s, then one H2 at the very end — **the four section titles ("The Numbers", "What We Do", "Why Us", "Trusted By") are `<p>` and `<div>`, not headings**, so the document has no navigable structure. No skip link past a 14.7-viewport page. With reduce enabled, 74 animations keep running.

## Minor Observations

- **Heading semantics** — `// THE NUMBERS`, `// WHAT WE DO`, `// WHY US`, `// TRUSTED BY` should be `<h2>`, styled as they are now. Fixes the outline for both screen readers and search.
- **CLS risk** — **all 16 `<img>` elements lack `width`/`height`**, including the 158KB `hero-bg.jpg`.
- **Lazy-loading gaps** — 14 of 16 images load eagerly, including three client logos, the China flag, three Steam logos and three service icons, all far below the fold.
- **Payload** — 750KB / 25 requests. `three.js` is **163KB (67% of all JS)** and was the slowest resource at 1071ms, for a decorative particle field. A hand-rolled canvas version would be ~3KB.
- **`contact-title` clamp max is 12rem** (~192px), double the 6rem display ceiling. It works as a closing statement, but it's the loudest element on the page by a wide margin.
- **`.nums-cap { max-width: 22ch }`** forces "visit-to-wishlist, a real / Steam store-page / optimization" into three ragged right-aligned lines.
- **Redundant labelling in Services** — `04 / 10` in the panel, `04` in the segment, "PERFORMANCE MARKETING" as the panel caption, and "PERFORMANCE MARKETING" as the H3 beside it. The panel caption is pure duplication of the heading two inches away.
- **The "magic" wordmark measures 2.05:1** against `--bg` at its gradient end (`#1E2FBE`). Logotypes are WCAG-exempt, and the particle field rescues it in the hero — but in the header and footer, over flat dark, half the company name is close to invisible.
- **No `404.html`** — GitHub Pages will serve its stock 404. `sitemap.xml` lists one URL.
- **162 elements carry inline styles** — the entire Services panel. Un-themeable, and it's where any future palette change will hurt.

## Questions to Consider

- What if the Numbers reel were three stats instead of six, and the 2,300px you got back became a case study?
- The copy is written by someone who has clearly run these campaigns. Why does the design never say who they are? No names, no faces, no team — for a service where the buyer is hiring *people*.
- What would this page look like if it opened at Services instead of arriving there on viewport seven?
- If a Bilibili partner opened this on a mainland connection right now, what would they see?
- The `//` is in the company name, the logo, the section labels and the marquee. What happens if it also becomes the interaction — the divider that draws, the focus ring, the cursor?
